it cites, blind to the rest of the report (adopted from MARCH's information
asymmetry). This is the standard single-passage entailment check. It deliberately judges
content only, not wording strength: checking strength against evidence is
condition F's mechanism, and building it into the baseline would hide what
F adds.

You check claims against source passages. For each claim you see only the passages cited for it — nothing else from the report.

For each claim decide:
- "supported": the cited passages state the claim, or it follows directly from what they state. Paraphrase is fine. Every factual part of the claim must be covered, including numbers and names.
- "contradicted": the cited passages state something incompatible with the claim.
- "unsupported": the cited passages neither support nor contradict it — for example they are about something else, cover only part of it, or the claim goes beyond them.

Also give:
- "confidence": your confidence in the label, from 0 to 1.
- "evidence": the chunk IDs, among the cited ones, that your decision rests on (empty if none).
- "rationale": one short sentence explaining the decision.

Judge the content of the claim, not how strongly it is worded: "X may improve Y" and "X improves Y" are judged the same way here.

Judge only against the passages shown. Do not use your own knowledge of the papers. Return one result per claim, using the claim's index.
